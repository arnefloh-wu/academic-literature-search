**Where the question sits.** "Differentiated integration" is an established construct in EU studies, not in alliance research. In EU studies it describes members integrating to different depths, speeds and scopes (Stubb's multi-speed, variable geometry and à la carte; Schimmelfennig, Leuffen and Rittberger's "system of differentiated integration"; Kölliker's club-goods explanation; Koremenos, Lipson and Snidal's flexibility provisions). Alliance research studies the same phenomenon under other names: parent control and autonomy in international joint ventures, alliance scope and partial or modular integration, governance tiers in multi-partner alliances and portfolios, and "selective integration" in airline alliances (Albers, van den Oord, Koch and Mandt 2025 is the only source found that names it in those words). The search therefore returned two bodies of work that have not yet been joined: a mature alliance-governance literature with the mechanisms, and a political-science literature with the construct, the typology and the measurement logic.
**Research streams found.**
- IJV control and autonomy (Geringer and Hebert 1989; Yan and Gray 1994; Inkpen and Beamish 1997; Luo, Shenkar and Nyaw 2001; Choi and Beamish 2004; Puck et al. 2009; Nippa and Reuer 2019): control is split by activity and by partner, which is differentiated integration at the dyad level, measured with survey scales in the 1990s and 2000s.
- Governance, contracts, trust and coordination (Das and Teng 2001; Poppo and Zenger 2002; Reuer and Ariño 2007; Hoetker and Mellewigt 2009; Gulati, Wohlgezogen and Zhelyazkov 2012; Schilke and Lumineau 2018; Reuer and Devarakonda 2016): the mechanisms through which integration depth is set and adjusted, including contractual complexity, board representation and relational governance.
- Scope and partial integration (Oxley and Sampson 2004; Li, Eden, Hitt and Ireland 2008; Albers, Wohlgezogen and Zajac 2016): alliances integrate only some activities, and the choice of scope is itself a design variable.
- Multi-partner alliances, portfolios and constellations (Das and Teng 2002; Lavie 2007; Hoffmann 2007; Wassmer 2010; Heidl, Steensma and Phelps 2014; Davis 2016; Fonti, Maoret and Whitbred 2017): the alliance-level counterpart of a union with members at different depths; airline alliances with membership tiers and antitrust-immunised metal-neutral joint ventures are the clearest empirical archetype (Kleymann and Seristö 2001; Lazzarini 2007; Bilotkach and Hüschelrath 2011; Zou and Chen 2017).
- The MNE analogy: integration-responsiveness and differentiated fit of subsidiaries (Ghoshal and Nohria 1989; Nohria and Ghoshal 1994; Birkinshaw and Morrison 1995; Rugman and Verbeke 2004) gives a ready vocabulary for "integration varies by unit and by activity".
- The conceptual origin: EU differentiated integration (Stubb 1996; Kölliker 2001; Holzinger and Schimmelfennig 2012; Schimmelfennig, Leuffen and Rittberger 2015; Schimmelfennig and Winzen 2020; Leuffen, Rittberger and Schimmelfennig 2022; Leruth, Gänzle and Trondal 2019) with its typology (vertical versus horizontal, internal versus external, temporary versus permanent), its explanations (heterogeneity of preferences and capacities, externalities, politicisation) and the EUDIFF datasets that measure it as a member-by-policy-by-year matrix.
**Theoretical lenses in use.** Transaction cost economics (Hennart 1988; Oxley 1997), the relational view and trust-control frameworks, agency and real-options reasoning about equity and board control (Reuer), network embeddedness (Gulati 1998; Ahuja 2000), organisation design and interdependence (Gulati et al. 2012; Albers et al. 2016), club theory and rational institutional design (Kölliker 2001; Koremenos et al. 2001), and loosely coupled systems for airline alliance groups.
**Methods and data in use.** Manager surveys of IJVs (cross-sectional, single-informant), SDC Platinum alliance panels with logit and survival models, meta-analyses (Reus and Rottig 2009; Christoffersen 2013), case studies and QCA (Fiss 2011), route-level fare and traffic panels with antitrust immunity as the treatment in the airline literature (US DOT T-100, OAG), and country-policy-year datasets in EU studies (EUDIFF1 and EUDIFF2; InDivEU). Endogeneity of governance choice is handled with switching regressions (Hamilton and Nickerson 2003; Shaver 1998).
**Gaps and opportunities.**
- No alliance study conceptualises differentiated integration as a property of a multi-partner international alliance; the typology and the measurement logic of EU studies (depth by member and by policy area over time) can be transferred directly.
- Measurement: build a partner-by-activity integration matrix for each alliance (membership tier, joint venture participation, revenue sharing, joint pricing, IT and loyalty integration, governance seats) and derive depth, breadth and dispersion indices, mirroring EUDIFF.
- Determinants: partner heterogeneity (size, home-market regulation, ownership caps, state ownership), institutional and cultural distance (Berry, Guillén and Zhou; CAGE), regulatory permission (antitrust immunity, open-skies agreements) and alliance age.
- Consequences: performance of partners at different integration tiers, spillovers between tiers, and the stability of differentiated versus uniform designs (exits and downgrades as "differentiated disintegration", the Brexit analogy).
- Data route: airline alliance membership and joint-venture histories from Star Alliance, oneworld and SkyTeam, US DOT and European Commission antitrust decisions, and T-100 or OAG traffic data; alternatively SDC Platinum portfolios coded by activity scope. Both allow a panel design that matches the researcher's quantitative profile.
**Five must-reads.**
- Schimmelfennig, Leuffen and Rittberger (2015), Journal of European Public Policy: the system-level theory and typology of differentiated integration.
- Albers, Wohlgezogen and Zajac (2016), Journal of Management: alliance structures as organisation design, with scope and integration as design dimensions.
- Albers, van den Oord, Koch and Mandt (2025), Elgar handbook chapter: governance, selective integration and networks of networks in airline alliances.
- Nippa and Reuer (2019), Journal of International Business Studies: where IJV control and governance research stands and what is open.
- Gulati, Wohlgezogen and Zhelyazkov (2012), Academy of Management Annals: cooperation and coordination as the two problems any integration design has to solve.
